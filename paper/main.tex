\title{\sysname: Topology-Aware Graph Contrastive Learning for Resilient Intrusion Detection}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Intrusion detectors must recognize evolving malicious behavior even when confirmed attack labels are scarce or incomplete. We present \sysname, a topology-aware graph contrastive learning framework for label-efficient intrusion detection. \sysname learns from benign system-activity graphs by combining message-passing and topological representations, then assigns anomaly scores in the same projected embedding space optimized by the contrastive objective. This design captures both local interactions and graph-scale structural deviations without using attack labels during representation learning. We evaluate \sysname on StreamSpot, GraSec-IoT, and Unicorn Wget against a supervised graph neural network and the benign-only GraphCL baseline. Relative to GraphCL, \sysname changes F1 from 0.923 to 0.926 on StreamSpot, from 0.790 to 0.985 on GraSec-IoT, and from 0.583 to 0.863 on Unicorn Wget. The StreamSpot difference is marginal, whereas the larger gains on GraSec-IoT and Unicorn Wget indicate that topology-aware representations can be valuable when malicious behavior produces detectable structural deviations. These results support topology-aware anomaly detection as a promising component of resilient ML-driven security systems under limited attack-label availability.
\end{abstract}
